The cost of voicemail

What a missed call costs a plumber

Someone with water on the floor usually doesn't leave a voicemail. They call the next plumber on the list.

Say you miss 5 calls a week, and 1 in 4 of them was a real job at a $400 average ticket. That's roughly $2,000 a month going to someone else. And that's before counting the big ones: a single missed water heater replacement is often $1,500 or more on its own.

How it works

How a plumbing call gets handled

1

Your phone rings first

You keep your number. Calls only forward to the AI when you don't pick up, so the customers you'd normally answer still get you.

2

It asks what a plumber would ask

Is water leaking right now? Where is it coming from: a pipe, the water heater, a drain, a fixture? Do they know where the main shutoff is? Then their address and the best number to reach them.

3

You get a text in seconds

A short summary lands on your phone, marked urgent or routine, so you can see at a glance who to call back first. You can text the customer back right from the business number.

If a caller hangs up before the AI picks up, they get a text a few seconds later so the job doesn't disappear.

Reputation

Reviews decide who gets the emergency call

Someone searching for an emergency plumber at night calls whoever looks most trustworthy on Google, and that usually means the most recent good reviews. ClymbUp watches your Google and Yelp reviews every day, alerts you the moment a bad one comes in with a reply drafted for you to approve, and texts customers a review request after the job is done.

Questions

Common questions

Can it tell a burst pipe from a dripping faucet?
It asks whether water is actively leaking, where it's coming from and how bad it is, then marks the call urgent or routine in the text you get. A burst pipe gets flagged; a slow drain doesn't jump the line.
Do I have to change my phone number?
No. Your number stays on your truck and your website. Your phone rings first, and only the calls you don't answer go to the AI.
Will customers know they're talking to an AI?
If they ask, it tells them it's your automated assistant and that you'll call them personally. What callers need is someone to pick up, take their details and tell them when they'll hear back, and that's what it does.
Does it handle Spanish-speaking callers?
Yes. If a caller starts speaking Spanish, it switches to Spanish for the rest of the call, and you still get the summary in English.
