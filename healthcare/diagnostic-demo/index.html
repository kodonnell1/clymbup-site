How the panel works

Five agents, each with one job

Microsoft Research's design gives each AI agent a single role on a virtual physician panel. The agents debate before every step: ask a question, order a test, or commit to a diagnosis.

  • Hypothesis: keeps a ranked list of the most likely diagnoses and updates it after every new finding.
  • Test-Chooser: picks the tests that best tell those diagnoses apart.
  • Challenger: plays devil's advocate, looking for evidence that contradicts the leading diagnosis.
  • Stewardship: pushes for the most cost-effective path and avoids low-value tests.
  • Checklist: checks that every order is valid and that the panel's reasoning stays consistent.

The research behind it

What Microsoft Research published

In June 2025, Microsoft Research tested its MAI Diagnostic Orchestrator (MAI-DxO) on 304 challenging case records from the New England Journal of Medicine, presented step by step the way a real diagnosis unfolds.

85.5%

Diagnostic accuracy at its highest-accuracy setting.

80% vs 20%

Accuracy in its standard setting, about four times the 20% average of the 21 physicians tested.

20% lower

Diagnostic testing costs than the physicians, and 70% lower than the same AI model used on its own.

Important context: the 21 physicians (5 to 20 years of experience, from the US and UK) worked without colleagues, textbooks or AI tools, which isn't how doctors normally practice. The cases were published puzzles, not live patients. Microsoft says the system has not been approved for clinical use and would first need safety testing, clinical validation and regulatory review.

Source: Nori et al., "Sequential Diagnosis with Language Models," Microsoft Research, 2025 (arXiv:2506.22405), and Microsoft AI, "The Path to Medical Superintelligence." ClymbUp is not affiliated with Microsoft.
